Good news: pressure only depends on the *height* of the water column above the sensor, not the tank's volume or width — so you don't need a big tank at all, just height.

**Math**: 1 psi ≈ 0.70 m of water head, so your 0–5psi sensor reads full-scale at ~3.5 m. A short, wide bucket gives you almost nothing to test with:
- 5-gallon bucket (~30cm/12in deep) → only ~0.3 psi, ~6% of range — too small a signal to verify much.

**What I'd actually do**: get a few meters of clear vinyl tubing (cheap, any hardware store) and mount it vertically — up a stairwell, taped to a wall, hung from a ladder, whatever's nearby. Connect the sensor fitting at the bottom, run the tube straight up, and fill with water from a jug at the top with a funnel. This needs almost no water (just whatever fills the tube's inner volume) but gives you real, known head:

- ~0.7m of tube → 1 psi (20% of scale)
- ~1.75m → 2.5 psi (50% of scale, good midpoint check)
- ~3.5m → 5 psi (full scale, if you can find that much vertical clearance — a stairwell atrium works)

Mark the tube with a tape measure at a few heights, fill to each mark, and compare the psi your code reports (via `debug#1` serial output from `readTankPressure1()`/`readTankPressure2()`) against the expected value from height × 0.70. For a manufactured product (not just a one-off test), 0.5–4.5V is the clear choice — and there's now an additional reason specific to your ADS1115-at-5V design that I hadn't covered yet:

**ADC input margin.** The ADS1115 datasheet's absolute max input is VDD + 0.3V. With VDD = 5V, that's a hard ceiling around 5.3V. A "0–5V" sensor sitting right at 5.0V output leaves you almost no margin — any supply sag/ripple on the ADS1115's 5V rail, or a sensor slightly out of spec, or a manufacturing unit that reads 5.1–5.2V, starts eating into that 0.3V buffer. A 0.5–4.5V sensor tops out at 4.5V, giving you a full 0.8V of headroom below the ADS1115's ceiling — much safer across a production run where you can't hand-verify every board's rail voltage.

**Two more reasons that matter more at volume than for a single test:**
- **Live-zero fault detection** (already discussed): at scale, "sensor died/disconnected" silently reading as "tank empty" turns into real support tickets and false alarms in the field. 0.5–4.5V lets firmware flag near-0V as a fault instead of an empty tank.
- **It's the actual industrial standard.** True 0–5V output pressure transducers are less common and often can't swing all the way to the rails anyway (op-amp output stage limits), so their real-world min/max is murky and inconsistent between suppliers/batches. 0.5–4.5V is a defined, guaranteed spec you'll get consistently from any vendor, which matters when you're sourcing in quantity.

Stick with 0.5–4.5V. No firmware change needed — that's what's already implemented.

Found it — and it's not a firmware bug in the switch logic, it's a design assumption that doesn't hold in practice. Traced the actual netlist (not just eyeballing the schematic, which I initially got wrong by ~2x):

## The circuit

```
DEVICE_POWER --R2(68.1k)-- CONFIG_SWITCH_OUT --R3(1k)--R4(2k)--R10(3.9k)--R11(8.2k)--R13(16k)-- GND
```
Switches SW1–SW5 each short across (bypass) one resistor when closed: SW1→R3, SW2→R4, SW3→R10, SW4→R11, SW5→R13 (the "solar" bit). This exactly matches the "1234 + solar" comment structure and your R3/R4/R10/R11/R13 naming — so the *logic* is right. The bug is in what `DEVICE_POWER` actually is.

## The bug

`DEVICE_POWER` feeds the ADS1115's VDD through a TPS2051B load switch — it's the **same unregulated solar/USB rail as V50_I**, per your own code comment: *"~0V at night, sags with cloud cover, ~5V+ in full sun."* The firmware's thresholds (`>=8300` for all-off, etc.) were clearly calculated assuming a fixed **5.00V** supply — at exactly 5.00V, "all switches off" computes to raw≈**8360**, matching the `>=8300` cutoff almost perfectly. But your device is reporting `V50Voltage: 4.16V` right now (not full sun/USB power), and the divider is ratiometric to that same sagging rail. At 4.16V, "all off" computes to raw≈**6956** — right in the neighborhood of your observed **6266** (the gap is plausibly resistor tolerance + the TPS2051B's small voltage drop under load).

6266 lands in `cswOutput >= 6100 && < 6300` (Position 9), which is one of the **undefined gaps** in the switch table — it never assigns `currentFunctionValue`, so it stays at its uninitialized default. That's exactly your blank "Current Function Value" in the debug printout.

## The real problem

The config switch reading will drift with sunlight/battery state, not just with switch position — the whole table shifts proportionally as `DEVICE_POWER` sags. Fixed ADC-count thresholds can't be reliable unless that rail is regulated.

The reboot loop is a **stack overflow**, not a hardware or power issue — confirmed by the clue that `debug` must already be `true` (since `"Entering COMMA mode..."` printed), yet the two later `goToSleep()` prints (`"Preparing sleep for..."`, `"Entering deep sleep."`) never appear — meaning the crash happens *before* those, inside `appendCommaRecord()`.
